Deep research · 2026 Formula 1 season

Faster driver, smarter pit wall, or lucky timing?

Two drivers start a race side by side. Ninety minutes later one is 25 seconds up the road. Ask where those seconds came from. A lead agent rebuilds the race lap by lap, sends researchers to investigate the laps where the gap moved, and a writer tells you which explanations the data supports and which it cannot confirm.

Who it's for

F1 fans who watched the race, heard the commentary, and want to check the story against the timing data without building a spreadsheet.

How to use it

Name a 2026 race and two drivers. Watch the team work in the research log. Click any E3 in the report to see the data behind that sentence.

What it knows

Lap times, tyre stints, pit stops and race-control messages from OpenF1; grids and results from Jolpica. Races only: no qualifying, sprints, telemetry or team radio.

Try
A full investigation takes about 30 to 60 seconds. Follow-up questions keep the same race and drivers.
